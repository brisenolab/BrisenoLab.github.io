# Project Report

Keep it brief, but include at least one sentence of the following structure.

## Structure:

* Objective (What was the goal?)
* Methods (What was done?)
* Results (What happened?)
* Discussion (What do the results mean?)
* Problems encountered (What didn’t work and why?)
* Next steps (What will be done next?)

Recommensations for the report.

1. Write for someone who was not in the lab.
    * Assume the reader knows the project, but not what happened that week.
    * The report should allow another scientist to understand exactly what was done.
2. Be precise, not verbose.
    * Replace vague statements like “I worked on RNA extraction” with:
        * “I extracted total RNA from 18 leaf samples using the Qiagen RNeasy Plant Mini Kit.”
3. Separate observations from interpretations.
    * Observation: Five of eight samples amplified the expected 450-bp fragment.
    * Interpretation: This suggests the target virus may be present, although sequencing is required for confirmation.
4. Report failures objectively.
    * Failed experiments are still data.
    * Include:
        * what was attempted,
        * what happened,
        * likely causes,
        * planned modifications.
5. Include enough methodological detail for reproducibility.
    * Sample IDs
    * Dates
    * Protocol version
    * Reagents or kits
    * Deviations from standard protocols
6. Present results before discussion.
    * Don’t mix what happened with what it might mean.
7. Quantify whenever possible.
    * Instead of:
        * Several cultures grew.
    * Write:
        * 12 of 15 cultures showed visible growth after 72 h.
8. State the next steps.
    * End each section with what logically follows from the results.
9. Use scientific rather than conversational language.
    * Avoid:
        * Luckily…
        * Unfortunately…
        * I think…
    * Prefer:
        * The experiment yielded…
        * These results indicate…
        * The data suggest…
10. Use figures and tables appropriately.
    * A good report should not rely only on text.
    * Include gels, plots, photos, or tables when they communicate the results more clearly.
11. Be honest about uncertainty.
    * Avoid overstating conclusions.
    * Distinguish between evidence and speculation.
12. Proofread before submission.
    * Consistent tense.
    * Consistent terminology.
    * Correct units and abbreviations.
    * Number figures and tables properly.
